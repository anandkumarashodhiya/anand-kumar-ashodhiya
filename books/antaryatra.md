## About the Book

**Antaryātrā — The Inner Journey** is a contemplative poetry collection that explores conscience, memory, and moral awareness in a fractured contemporary world. Rather than offering ornamental verse, this work traces a sustained inward movement—an ethical pilgrimage through love and loss, violence and compassion, dissent and devotion. Each poem functions as a moment of pause, inviting readers to confront the inner echoes of lived experience.

### Moral Witnessing and Social Consciousness
Anand Kumar Ashodhiya’s verse engages directly with social realities and historical memory without surrendering to rhetoric or spectacle. Poems such as *Danga* and *Kargil* stand as acts of moral witnessing, addressing communal rupture and national sacrifice with restraint, clarity, and dignity. Patriotism is rendered as disciplined reverence rather than performance; protest emerges as ethical insistence rather than noise.

### Intimate Lyricism and Inner Inquiry
Alongside this social awareness runs a grounded human lyricism. Love, separation, remembrance, and longing appear not as romantic excess, but as quiet aftershocks of intimacy. Personal emotion expands into philosophical inquiry, allowing individual sorrow to assume universal resonance. 

Stylistically, the collection bridges the earthiness of regional idiom with the refined poise of meditative verse. The language remains unadorned yet resonant, capable of carrying folk immediacy alongside philosophical depth.
