📖 Abstract

This research paper presents a comprehensive and critically grounded analysis of the Haryanvi Saang-style raginis (Ragini 1–8) from the narrative series Adharajan, examined through literary, prosodic, and socio-cultural perspectives. The study investigates narrative structure, emotional depth, gendered consciousness, and metrical organization within the framework of Pingal Shastra.

The study argues that Haryanvi ragni is not merely an oral folk expression but a structured poetic tradition reflecting a synthesis of indigenous aesthetics and classical poetics. The sequence of Ragini 1–8 follows a coherent narrative progression—from aesthetic awareness and emotional awakening to courtly interaction, intensification of love, social tension, and assertion of dignity.

The character of Raskapoor emerges as a central figure representing feminine agency and ethical consciousness, while Sawai Jagat Singh embodies the tension between royal authority and human vulnerability. Prosodically, the compositions demonstrate the use of mixed syllabic metres, balanced caesura, patterned rhyme schemes, and rhythmic recurrence.

The study concludes that these raginis should be recognized as culturally significant literary texts contributing to academic discourse in Indian folk literature and cultural studies.
