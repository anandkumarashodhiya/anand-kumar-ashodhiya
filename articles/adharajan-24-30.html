📖 Abstract

This study presents an integrated analysis of Ragni 24–30 from the Haryanvi saang tradition centered on the narrative of Adharajan (Raskapoor), examined through the frameworks of Pingal prosody and folk-cultural interpretation. Moving beyond a localized romantic narrative, the study reveals the structural, emotional, and ideological depth of these compositions, demonstrating how oral poetic forms articulate complex dimensions of power, ethics, gender identity, and cultural memory.

The narrative progression across the selected ragnis is interpreted as a sequential movement from unjust captivity and emotional rupture to symbolic resistance, existential wandering, political awareness, introspective suffering, and ultimate transcendence through death. This progression transforms the narrative from a personal love story into a culturally embedded discourse of feminine resilience and ethical assertion.

From a prosodic standpoint, the study identifies the consistent use of the “4+6+8+10” metrical pattern in alignment with Pingal principles, where rhythmic progression corresponds to emotional intensification. The recurrence of refrains, internal rhyme schemes, and phonetic patterns is analysed as functional devices that support memory, performance, and audience participation within oral traditions.

Thematically, Raskapoor emerges as a figure of resistance within feudal and patriarchal constraints, while the inner conflict of King Jagat Singh reflects the fragility of authority under political pressure. The study concludes that Haryanvi saang ragnis represent a significant yet understudied domain in Indian literary scholarship, offering critical insights into gender discourse, performative traditions, and subaltern expression.
